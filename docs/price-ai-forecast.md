# Price AI — the main-chart forecast · Atlas implementation notes

Atlas's main-chart AI used to answer _"what is the tape doing right now"_. It now also answers the
question a trader actually has to act on: **over the next N bars, where does price go, and does it
finish above or below the strike line drawn on the chart?** Every number below is printed next to
the call it belongs to; none of it is a black box, and all of it is graded against what later
printed.

- Engine: `src/lib/price-forecast.ts`
- Specialists, director, learning: `src/lib/market-agents.ts`
- Journal (record → settle → learn): `src/lib/agent-journal.ts`
- Pre-trained prior: `src/lib/agent-pretrained.ts`, generated by `src/lib/agent-training.test.ts`
- Window: `src/components/AgentDecisionBox.tsx` · Panel: `src/components/AgentPanel.tsx`

## The question

| Piece      | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Horizon    | Normally a fixed number of bars from the anchor (the last **closed** candle). Default 10 on 1m–15m, 8 on 1h, 6 on 4h, 5 on 1D, 4 on 1W; the panel can change it per timeframe. When the timeframe-peek window is open and points to the chart timeframe or a higher timeframe, the live horizon is temporarily synced to that peek bar's close instead. |
| Anchor     | The close of the last closed candle — the price everything is measured from.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Strike     | The level line the chart is showing: the Coinbase strike indicator's live level (interval open, or the custom price when one is set), else the defended 15-minute window strike.                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Settlement | At `anchor + horizonBars`, or at the strike window's cut — the panel chooses. Entries settle on the close of the first candle at or after the target time, never mid-bar.                                                                                                                                                                               |

The call is the **finish side**: `above` / `below` the pinned strike, with the probability of that
side attached. When neither side clears the coin-flip band the call is `none` and the journal says
so; an abstention is never scored as a win.

### Peek-synced timing

When Peek is visible, Price AI derives its effective horizon from the peek resolution instead of
blindly reusing the saved N-bar setting. Example: on a 1m chart with a 15m peek bar that opened at
9:00 and the chart anchored on 9:07, the forecast becomes an 8-bar forecast so the finish lands at
the 9:15 peek close. The side panel still keeps the saved horizon as a fallback; hide Peek, or pin
Peek below the chart timeframe, and the saved setting takes over again. If a manually pinned peek
resolution does not share closes with the chart grid, the forecast snaps to the first chart close
after the peek close and the UI labels it as a peek-synced horizon.

## How a specialist becomes a forecast

Each of the eight specialists keeps its existing read — the same score, confidence, reasons and
warnings it uses for the present-tense verdict — and gains a horizon projection built from it:

1. **Drift.** `driftAtr = clamp(score × carryAtr × √(persistence / horizon), ±0.35 × √horizon)`.
   `carryAtr` is how much of a move that agent's evidence is worth across the horizon; a fast read
   decays because it does not last (whale 2 bars, momentum 5, MACD 7), a slow one does not (trend
   30, structure 30, higher-timeframe context 60). The cap stops any single agent from inventing a
   move larger than a third of the excursion a horizon's own noise implies.

   | Specialist     | carry (ATR) | persistence (bars) |
   | -------------- | ----------- | ------------------ |
   | regime         | 0.85        | 18                 |
   | trend          | 1.15        | 30                 |
   | momentum       | 0.80        | 5                  |
   | macd           | 0.85        | 7                  |
   | level-strength | 0.70        | 30                 |
   | structure      | 0.70        | 30                 |
   | whale          | 0.50        | 2                  |
   | context        | 1.05        | 60                 |

2. **The strike question.** With Δ the distance to the strike in ATR (positive = price above) and
   μ the projected drift in ATR:

   `P(finish above) = Φ((Δ + μ) / √bars)`

   One ATR per bar is the volatility stand-in, so `√bars` is the horizon's move scale. A tape
   sitting on the strike with no drift is exactly 50%.

3. **Will the strike be traded through?** A different question from where the close lands, so it
   gets a different number: the first-passage probability of a drifting walk reaching a barrier
   `|Δ|` away within `bars`,

   `P(touch) = Φ((μ̂h − a)/√h) + exp(2μ̂a) · Φ((−μ̂h − a)/√h)`

   with `μ̂` the per-bar drift measured **toward** the barrier and `a` the distance. `strikeBars` is
   the first bar by which that probability passes one half — a timing estimate derived from the same
   number, not a second model.

4. **Hold or break.** What the strike does when it is reached: the projected overshoot past the
   level, against how much funding sits on that price — level strength from fill density, touches
   and volume (SR zones first, pivots as fallback), reinforced by the resting order book on that
   side. A touch that carries well past an unfunded level is a **break**; a touch into a funded wall
   is a **hold**.

5. **Path and thrust.** The ensemble's shape for the horizon: `continuation`, `reversal` (slow trend
   against fast opposition), `range`, or `squeeze` (current ATR compressed against the recent bar
   range). Thrust is `strong` / `mild` / `fade` by the size of the projected drift.

6. **Levels in the way.** The same first-passage math prices the chance that the nearest confirmed
   support and resistance are reached inside the horizon — the two numbers that usually decide
   whether the finish is realistic.

## The director

The projections are combined with the same weights the live ensemble uses — the regime table times
each specialist's learned trust times a horizon tilt (`√(6/bars)` for the fast readers,
`√(bars/8)` for the slow ones). The ensemble projection is the weighted mean drift, and everything
the window shows is derived from it: expected close, the ±1 scale band, the strike probability, the
touch probability, the path, the timeline and the confidence.

**Timeline.** The window and the panel both lead with the order things are expected to happen in,
borrowed from MACD AI: near bars (test the strike, or hold the side), the moment of contact (break
or hold, and why), then the finish bar with the expected close and band. When there is no strike
line, the middle step becomes the levels in the way.

## Learning and the journal

Every closed candle records one entry: the pinned strike, the horizon, the projected drift, the
call and its probability. When the horizon completes, the entry settles against what actually
printed:

- `actualStrikeSide` — the finish side, with a flat band of `0.1 ATR` around the strike;
- `strikeResult` — `correct` / `wrong`, recorded only when the forecast chose a side;
- `touchedStrike` — whether the level was traded through inside the horizon;
- `actualDriftAtr` — the realized move in the forecast's own units.

That becomes a skill score per agent, split three ways so no component can dominate:
45% directional utility, 55% projection quality — drift error measured against the horizon's own
move scale, probability calibration scored as `0.5 + (0.75 − Brier) × 2` (perfect call 1, coin flip
0.5, confident miss 0), and the touch call when it was genuinely uncertain (a predicted touch
probability between 0.15 and 0.85 — a strike five ATR away "not touched" is not evidence of skill).
Cells are per agent, per regime and per timeframe, updated as an exponential moving average, and
they only ever scale trust between 0.55× and 1.4× — the AI adapts its confidence in its readers,
not its facts about the market.

## The pre-trained prior

A fresh install does not start empty. `src/lib/agent-training.test.ts` replays real Coinbase
BTC-USD history through the exact production loop — analyze, journal, settle, learn — and writes
`src/lib/agent-pretrained.ts`, so the first forecast already carries trust learned from the tape:

- strictly causal: a forecast at bar _i_ sees only `candles[0..i]`, the strike that was live then,
  and higher-timeframe context rebuilt by aggregating the same series into **closed** buckets only;
- the strike is reconstructed the way a trader would have it on that resolution — the 15-minute
  window on 1m/5m/15m charts, 4 hours on 1h, a week on 1D — as the open of the candle that opened
  the window;
- learning accumulates in chronological order, so the prior is the end state of that replay.

The harness prints its own report card (strike-call accuracy, Brier score, drift error, touch
accuracy, and first-half vs second-half accuracy so a cold-start prior is not mistaken for skill)
and asserts behavioral floors before it writes anything — a regression cannot bless new weights.
Its last recorded run, on the bundled scenarios:

| 1h Nov-2024 rally | 228       | 69.8%       | 0.199 | 1.48 ATR    | 73.4%             |
| 1h recent         | 289       | 73.0%       | 0.209 | 1.32 ATR    | 74.8%             |

| 1m recent         | 289       | 76.3%       | 0.164 | 2.09 ATR    | 75.2%             |

**Read that table honestly.** These are selected historical windows, not out-of-sample guarantees,
and the strike-call hit rate is dominated by the distance case: a tape already three ATR from the
strike usually finishes on its side, which is arithmetic rather than foresight. The number that
carries information is the Brier score — 0.25 is a coin flip, and every scenario lands below it,
which is what "the printed percentage means something" has to look like. The drift error column is
the honest cost: over ten bars of 1m tape (√10 ≈ 3.2 ATR of noise) the projection misses the
realized move by 2.1 ATR on average.

## Limitations, stated

- **It is a model.** A Gaussian walk with ATR as the per-bar scale: no fat tails, no gap risk, no
  liquidation cascades, no news, no regime break inside the horizon. `√bars` scaling is an
  assumption, and it is the assumption everything above rests on.
- **Strike pinned, not re-based.** The entry is graded against the strike that was on screen when the
  forecast was made. If the horizon runs past an interval roll, the call is about the level the user
  was looking at, and the entry says so. When the strike window is narrower than one candle, the AI
  says out loud that the line resets every bar.
- **Nothing is resampled.** Context timeframes are real aggregated series, never a silently
  resampled chart. Where history is too short for a context frame, the context agent simply is not
  there.
- **Local and read-only.** Learning lives in this browser, resets are explicit (pre-trained prior or
  a fresh install), demo mode has no whale flow, and no order is placed or suggested. A probability
  is not advice.
